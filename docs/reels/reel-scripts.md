# Faceless reel scripts

The founder does not appear on camera. Everything here is shot with hands, a
screen, or nothing at all — no face in any frame.

## The honest trade-off

A face is the strongest retention device on Reels, and giving it up costs
reach. It does not cost credibility, which is the part that matters here: what
this brand sells is a published price and a checkable number, and both of those
are things you show rather than things you say. The substitute for a face is
**a voice and a pair of hands** — human presence without identity. If the voice
is available too, take it; a real voice recovers most of what the face gave up.

Ranked by expected performance, all faceless:

1. Hands + screen + voiceover — closest to the face version.
2. Live teardown of a real report — suspense does the work.
3. Hands + screen, text only, no voice — works, reaches least.

**On synthetic voice:** do not use a text-to-speech read for this brand. A
listener who spots it will discount every honesty claim in the script, and this
brand has nothing to fall back on if the trust positioning goes. Real voice or
no voice.

## A note on register

`CLAUDE.md` sets an elevated, formal B2B voice for written customer-facing copy.
**Spoken and on-screen video copy is the exception.** Read those sentences over
a reel and it sounds like a brochure being read aloud, which undercuts the exact
positioning it is meant to carry. Video register is short, plain and direct —
the honesty is unchanged, only the sentence length is.

# Reel 1 — "The question nobody answers"

~23 seconds. Hands and screen only. Shoot the screen with a second phone from
above, or screen-record and cut the hand shots in between.

## Version A — with voiceover (recommended)

| Time | Shot | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Close on hands, typing into a phone: *how much does an seo agency cost* | "Type this into Google, and you will get a hundred articles and not one price." |
| 0:04–0:09 | Thumb scrolling results, fast. Do not linger on any brand name. | "Every agency page ends the same way. Request a quote. Book a call. Let's talk." |
| 0:09–0:14 | Cut to the pricing page on a laptop, scrolling the tier row. | "Ours ends with a number. Sixteen thousand rupees a month to start, every service, every tier, on the page." |
| 0:14–0:19 | Hands dragging a slider in the plan builder; the total updates. | "You can build the plan yourself and see the total before you message me." |
| 0:19–0:23 | Tools page, a scan running, the score landing. | "Twelve free checks on your own site. clicknlikes.com." |

About 70 words. Record the voiceover separately, sitting still, phone at an
arm's length — do not narrate while filming, the mic will pick up the desk.

## Version B — no voice, text on screen

Same shots. Each line appears as one short caption, held for its whole beat:

- **0:00–0:04** — "Google 'how much does an SEO agency cost'"
- **0:04–0:09** — "A hundred articles. Not one price."
- **0:09–0:14** — "Ours is on the page. From ₹16,000/mo."
- **0:14–0:19** — "Build the plan. See the total. No call."
- **0:19–0:23** — "12 free checks on your own site → clicknlikes.com"

Keep to one line at a time, big, high contrast, bottom third clear of the
Instagram caption area.

## How to shoot it

- **The over-the-shoulder screen shots** are the whole texture. Desk, one lamp
  or a window, phone or laptop flat, second phone above it. Wipe the screen
  first; fingerprints show at this size.
- **Move deliberately.** Slow, decided scrolling reads as confident; frantic
  scrolling reads as nervous.
- **No stock footage.** Every frame is our real site or a real search.
- **Captions always**, even on Version A. Most of it is watched on mute.

## Alternate hooks

1. "Type 'how much does an SEO agency cost' into Google. Count the prices."
   *(in the script)*
2. "Ask an agency what they charge. Watch what happens."
3. "Nobody publishes agency pricing. Here is ours."

## Caption

> Every agency will book you a call before they'll give you a number. Ours is on
> the website instead — every service, every tier, from ₹16,000/month.
>
> Build the plan yourself, see the total, and decide whether we're worth it
> before you ever message us. That's the whole idea.
>
> Prices and 12 free checks: clicknlikes.com

# Reel 2 — the live teardown

~25 seconds, screen recording only. This format works because the viewer wants
to know the score. That is real suspense, and it advertises a tool that captures
an email.

## Shot list

| Time | What is on screen | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Already typing a URL into the Website Health Scan. Start mid-action — no page load, no intro. | "Let's score a real website." |
| 0:03–0:07 | Hit scan. The dial animates. The number lands. | The score, big. |
| 0:07–0:16 | Scroll the report slowly, pausing on three failures. Zoom into each. | "No meta description." · "3 H1s." · "Core Web Vitals: poor." |
| 0:16–0:21 | Cut to the fixed version, or to the summary. | "Every one of these is a week of work." |
| 0:21–0:25 | The tools page. | "Run yours free — clicknlikes.com" |

## Before you post this: do not score a named business

Publicly scoring an identifiable company's website and calling it broken is a
real risk — commercially and legally. It reads as disparagement, it makes every
prospect wonder whether you will do it to them, and in India a business whose
site you publicly graded has a defamation angle if any part of your framing is
arguably unfair. The upside is a few thousand views. The downside is a letter.

Three safe versions of the same reel:

- **Your own earlier site.** Strongest option — self-deprecating, honest, and
  nobody can object.
- **A client's before-state, with written permission**, framed as a case study
  rather than a takedown.
- **A blurred domain.** Blur the URL bar and never name the business. Say "a
  site I was sent this week."

## Caption

> This takes 30 seconds and it is free. Most sites we scan fail on the same
> three things — the title tag, the meta description, and mobile speed. None of
> them are hard to fix; they just never get looked at.
>
> Scan your own: clicknlikes.com/tools

# What to post, and where

- **Reel 1** to Instagram Reels, Facebook and LinkedIn. LinkedIn matters most:
  the audience there is the buyer.
- **Reel 2** to Reels and TikTok, where the format's suspense does the work.
- **The rendered launch reel** as the pinned profile video and a LinkedIn post.
  It is a credibility asset, not a reach play.
