# Two reels worth more than the launch reel

The rendered launch reel is a good pinned brand video. Neither of these needs
rendering, and both will outperform it, because one has a face in it and the
other has suspense in it.

## A note on register

`AGENTS.md` sets an elevated, formal B2B voice for written customer-facing
copy. **Spoken video is the exception.** Read those sentences aloud to a phone
camera and it sounds like a man reading a brochure, which is exactly the thing
the brand claims not to be. Spoken register is short, plain and direct — the
honesty is unchanged, only the sentence length is.

# Reel 1 — "Nobody will tell you what they charge"

~22 seconds. Phone, vertical, one take. This is the highest-return thing you
can film, because the product being sold is your willingness to say it out loud.

## Script, word for word

> **[0:00–0:03 — straight to camera, no greeting, no "hey guys"]**
> Call ten marketing agencies today. Not one of them will tell you what they
> charge.
>
> **[0:03–0:08]**
> You'll get a discovery call. A questionnaire. A proposal in five days. And a
> number you can't compare to anybody else's.
>
> **[0:08–0:13 — hold your phone up, pricing page on screen]**
> We put ours on the website. Every service. Every tier. Starting at sixteen
> thousand rupees a month.
>
> **[0:13–0:18 — phone down, back to camera]**
> Not because we're cheap. Because you should be able to work out whether we're
> worth it before you ever speak to me.
>
> **[0:18–0:22]**
> clicknlikes.com. The prices are right there. So are twelve free checks you can
> run on your own site.

About 75 words. At a natural pace that lands near 22 seconds. If it runs long,
cut the questionnaire line — not the last line.

## How to shoot it

- **Framing:** vertical, chest up, camera at eye level. Not above you, not on a
  desk pointing up.
- **Light:** face a window. That is the whole lighting setup. Never sit with the
  window behind you.
- **Sound:** indoors, soft furnishings in the room, phone about an arm's length
  away. Phone mics are fine at that distance; they are not fine across a room.
- **Delivery:** talk to one person, not an audience. Slightly faster than feels
  comfortable. Do not smile into the first line — it is a complaint, not a
  greeting.
- **The prop matters.** Physically holding the phone up with the pricing page on
  it is the moment that proves the claim. Practise the hand-off so it lands on
  "we put ours on the website", not two seconds late.
- **Takes:** shoot it five times, keep take three or four. The first two are
  always stiff.
- **Captions:** add them in the Instagram editor. Most of this will be watched
  on mute, and the hook has to land as text as well as speech.

## Alternate hooks, worth testing

1. "Call ten marketing agencies today. Not one will tell you what they charge."
   *(in the script)*
2. "Ask an agency what they charge. Watch what happens."
3. "I published my agency's prices. Here is why almost nobody does."

## Caption

> Every agency will book you a call before they'll give you a number. We put
> ours on the website instead — every service, every tier, from ₹16,000/month.
>
> You should be able to decide whether we're worth it before you ever speak to
> me. That's the whole idea.
>
> Prices and 12 free checks: clicknlikes.com

# Reel 2 — the live teardown

~25 seconds, no face needed, entirely a screen recording. This format works
because the viewer wants to know the score. That is real suspense, and it is
advertising a tool that captures an email.

## Shot list

| Time | What is on screen | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Already typing a URL into the Website Health Scan. Start mid-action — no page loading, no intro. | "Let's score a real website." |
| 0:03–0:07 | Hit scan. The dial animates. The number lands. | The score, big. |
| 0:07–0:16 | Scroll the report slowly, pausing on three failures. Zoom into each. | "No meta description." · "3 H1s." · "Core Web Vitals: poor." |
| 0:16–0:21 | Cut to the fixed version, or to the summary. | "Every one of these is a week of work." |
| 0:21–0:25 | The tools page. | "Run yours free — clicknlikes.com" |

## Before you post this: do not score a named business

Publicly scoring an identifiable company's website and calling it broken is a
real risk — commercially and legally. It reads as disparagement, it makes every
prospect wonder whether you will do it to them, and in India a business whose
site you publicly graded has a defamation angle if any part of your framing is
arguably unfair. The upside is a few thousand views. The downside is a letter.

Three safe versions of the same reel:

- **Your own earlier site.** Strongest option — self-deprecating, honest, and
  nobody can object.
- **A client's before-state, with written permission**, framed as a case study
  rather than a takedown.
- **A blurred domain.** Blur the URL bar and never name the business. Say "a
  site I was sent this week."

## Caption

> This takes 30 seconds and it is free. Most sites we scan fail on the same
> three things — the title tag, the meta description, and mobile speed. None of
> them are hard to fix; they just never get looked at.
>
> Scan your own: clicknlikes.com/tools

# What to post, and where

- **Reel 1** to Instagram Reels, Facebook and LinkedIn. LinkedIn matters most:
  the audience there is the buyer.
- **Reel 2** to Reels and TikTok, where the format's suspense does the work.
- **The rendered launch reel** as the pinned profile video and a LinkedIn post.
  It is a credibility asset, not a reach play.
