# Click.n.likes Growth Roadmap: ₹0 → ₹1L → ₹10L/month

Two stages, handed off deliberately rather than blended. The mistake this
document exists to prevent: reaching for ₹10L-stage tools (dashboards,
modelled unit economics, hands-off systems) while still below ₹1L/month,
which solves next year's problem before this year's. As of writing, the
agency is **below ₹1L/month** — so we are in Stage 0, and Stage 1 stays
parked until Stage 0's exit trigger actually fires.

## Stage 0: ₹0 → ₹1L/month — the founder finds the customer

This is the stage where founder effort, direct outreach and gut feel are not
a compromise — they are the correct tool for this size, exactly as the
₹1L→₹10L framework itself says. The mistake to avoid here is over-building:
we already have a full site rebuild, 9+ free tools, an auto-blog, a citation
kit, schema tools. Genuinely useful assets. None of them close a deal by
themselves at this size.

**01 — Mindset: from "build the system" to "have the next conversation
yourself"**
The instinct that produced the current infrastructure (tools, content, a
rebuilt site) is not wrong, it is just finished for this stage. The unlock
now is using what already exists to have more sales conversations
personally, not building more of it. The Hunter.io-powered prospecting we
just wired up is exactly this lever: one founder, finding and personally
pitching one real decision-maker at a time. That is the whole job at this
stage.

**02 — Data: from "no tracking" to "one number — conversations booked this
week"**
Site traffic, tool completions, blog pageviews: all leading indicators, none
of them revenue. The only number worth checking weekly right now is real
sales conversations had (calls, DMs, replies that go somewhere), because at
this volume it is countable by hand and directly causal to the next deal.
Site analytics start mattering once there is enough volume that "just look
at it" stops working — that is a Stage 1 problem, not a Stage 0 one.

**03 — Economics: from "undefined" to "one real, sourced case study per
vertical actually being pitched"**
Not unit economics yet — proof. AidByLaw is the one real, numbers-backed
case study live today (`/work/aidbylaw/`), plus two website-only builds
(Adamas Films, and LumaGreens/Ramat Home once written). The ₹0→₹1L unlock is
having a genuine, sourced case study for each vertical actually being
cold-pitched, because a founder pitching a stranger with zero comparable
proof is the single biggest drag on conversion at this stage — worse than
any pricing or positioning problem.

**Positioning at this stage:** pick 2–3 verticals to pitch into deliberately
per prospecting run, rather than "we do everything for everyone." The site
can stay broad (it already serves SaaS, manufacturing, legal, D2C, local,
film/media); outbound pitching should not — match the vertical being pitched
to whichever case study actually proves it.

### The handoff trigger (do not move to Stage 1 before this is real)

Consistent ₹1L+/month for **2–3 consecutive months**, not one good month —
and the founder has become the bottleneck (every hour spent selling is an
hour not spent delivering). Both conditions, not either.

## Stage 1: ₹1L → ₹10L/month — the system finds the customer

Parked until the trigger above fires. Kept here so the handoff is planned,
not improvised in the moment.

**01 — Mindset: from "I find the customer" to "the system finds the
customer"**
The direct-outreach, founder-network approach that works at ₹1L cannot
generate ₹10L — there are not enough personal hours in a month. This is the
point where the prospecting workflow (Hunter.io discovery + personalized
pitch) needs to stop being something the founder runs by hand and become
something that runs with less founder input per lead: templated by vertical,
higher volume, less bespoke per email.

**02 — Data: from "gut feel" to "weekly diagnostics"**
At ₹1L, intuition is efficient: small scale, low cost of a wrong call. At
₹10L, the same intuition is deciding what to do with ₹5–10L/month of
committed spend and delivery capacity on incomplete information. This is
where the site's existing lead data (tool completions, form fills, which
service pages convert) actually needs a weekly review cadence instead of
living unexamined in Google Sheets.

**03 — Economics: from "roughly profitable" to "unit economics modelled and
defended"**
"Rough sense" stops being enough, because ₹10L with an unmeasured contribution
margin can mean very little real cash. What this means concretely for an
agency, not a product business: cost to actually deliver each retainer type
(hours + tools + any subcontracted work) modelled against what it bills, per
service line, so scaling decisions are made against real margin rather than
top-line revenue.

## The throughline

Almost nothing that gets the agency to ₹1L is sufficient to take it to ₹10L.
The gap closes by deliberately replacing founder effort with systems, gut
feel with data, and broad positioning with per-vertical specificity — but
only once Stage 0 is actually, verifiably done. Building Stage 1 early does
not accelerate Stage 0; it distracts from it.
