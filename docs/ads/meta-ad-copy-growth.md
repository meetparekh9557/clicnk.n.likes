# Meta ad copy — /growth/ campaign

Copy for the two creatives running into one ad set: the 15-second video and
the static (4:5, 9:16, 1.91:1).

Framework, per `docs/content-frameworks.md`: Pixar beats, tighter. The same
structure the scripts use, compressed into a feed post.

## Three rules this copy is written to

**The first 125 characters are the whole ad.** Meta truncates the rest behind
"See more" on mobile and most people never open it. Every primary text below
lands its argument inside the first two lines. Longest is 240 characters.

**No em dashes.** House style for ad copy. Commas, full stops or a restructure
instead.

**The copy must not repeat the creative.** The video and the static already
say *your business doesn't need more marketing*. Primary text that says it
again wastes the one place you get to add something, so the copy answers the
question the creative provokes: fine, how would I know which part is broken?

## Slots

Ads Manager takes **5 primary texts, 5 headlines and 1 description** for this
format. Meta mixes and matches the texts and headlines, so every headline has
to read under every primary text. None of them below depend on a specific body.

## Notes

**Subtitles: don't add any.** The video is kinetic typography, so the words are
already on screen at phone size. Burned-in captions would print the same
sentence twice in one frame. Turn Meta's auto-captions off for this creative
too; they sit in the lower third where the CTA lands.

**Spelling.** Every form on the site says **Enquiry**; the creative's button
says `INQUIRY`. The copy above uses the site's spelling. Worth settling one way
and making the button match, since a viewer sees both within seconds.

**No statistics anywhere, on purpose.** Nothing here needs a number to work,
and any number would have to be one of the real sourced client figures, which
carries a client-approval requirement this copy doesn't otherwise need. See
`docs/ads/meta-video-ads-growth.md`.

**Claims to keep true.** "Every price published" is checkable on `/pricing/`.
Primary text 4 promises to tell people what deserves attention even when it
earns us nothing, which the sales call has to actually honour. It is the
strongest line in the set and the easiest to accidentally make a lie.
